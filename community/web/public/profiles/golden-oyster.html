Culinary Profile

Golden Oyster

Pleurotus citrinopileatus, Golden

Golden Oyster fruits at 21 °C and 87% humidity with plenty of fresh air and 12 h light. About 24 days from start to fruit.

Warm oyster with dense golden clusters. Needs plenty of fresh air to keep stems short.

Grow parameters

  1. Colonization 12 days, 25 °C, 90% humidity
  2. Pinning 4 days, 22 °C, 93% humidity
  3. Fruiting 8 days, 21 °C, 87% humidity

    More light helps hold gold color.

Light recipe

P. citrinopileatus has no variety-specific lux in the sources below. The recipe is the P. ostreatus oyster default and is labeled as such.

WS2812 blue is roughly 465 to 475 nm. The RGB mix is an approximation of cited wavelengths, not a measured spectrum.

Recipe sources

  • Penn State oyster production. Daniel J. Royse, Cultivation of Oyster Mushrooms, Penn State College of Agricultural Sciences. First 12 to 21 days of spawn run may be completed without artificial lighting. After spawn run, 4 h of cool white fluorescent light at 50 to 300 lux may be provided. This recipe uses 12 h as a labeled default photoperiod, not a page-cited Stamets value, and maps 20% white to 92 lux, inside that 50 to 300 lux band.
  • Richartz and MacLellan 1987 action spectrum. Gisela Richartz and Andrew J. MacLellan, Action spectra for hyphal aggregation, the first stage of fruiting, in Pleurotus ostreatus, Photochemistry and Photobiology 45 (1987). Peaks at 370 nm and 440 to 450 nm. Wavelengths longer than 530 nm gave no response.
  • De Bonis 2024 LED wavelengths. Marina De Bonis et al., Effect of Different LED Light Wavelengths on Production and Quality of Pleurotus ostreatus, Horticulturae 10 (2024), with Penn State co-authors. 450 nm blue increased cap diameter versus red 610 nm.
  • Stamets. Paul Stamets, Growing Gourmet and Medicinal Mushrooms, Ten Speed Press. Used here as a general cultivation reference. This recipe does not take a photoperiod or lux number from a cited page in that book.
  • General default. No variety-specific photoperiod or lux was found for this species. Colonization is dark (Sakamoto 2018; Penn State oyster spawn run). Fruiting uses 12 h as a labeled default, not a page-cited Stamets value, and 20% white, which is 92 lux on this chamber, inside the Penn State oyster 50 to 300 lux band. Blue 80% approximates the 440 to 470 nm finding. This is a labeled default, not a measured value for this variety.
