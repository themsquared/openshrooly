Culinary Profile

King Oyster

Pleurotus eryngii, King

King Oyster fruits at 15 °C and 82% humidity with moderate fresh air and 12 h light. About 38 days from start to fruit.

Thick-stem oyster. Cooler, slightly drier, and less fresh air than cluster oysters.

Grow parameters

  1. Colonization 18 days, 22 °C, 88% humidity
  2. Pinning 6 days, 16 °C, 90% humidity
  3. Fruiting 14 days, 15 °C, 82% humidity

    Let stems elongate before caps flare if you want steak-cut kings.

Light recipe

Dark while it colonizes, then 12 h a day of cool white with some blue.

Light details

Same dark spawn run as other oysters. Fruiting is blue-heavy because P. eryngii primordia respond to blue light. Hours and white percent are the oyster default (12 h, 20% / 92 lux). No king-oyster-specific lux was found.

WS2812 blue is roughly 465 to 475 nm. The RGB mix is an approximation of cited wavelengths, not a measured spectrum.

Sources
  • Xie 2018 king oyster. Chunliang Xie et al., Comparative transcriptomics of Pleurotus eryngii reveals blue-light regulation of carbohydrate-active enzymes (CAZymes) at primordium differentiated into fruiting body stage, Genomics 110 (2018). Blue light induced primordium differentiation.
  • Penn State oyster production. Daniel J. Royse, Cultivation of Oyster Mushrooms, Penn State College of Agricultural Sciences. First 12 to 21 days of spawn run may be completed without artificial lighting. After spawn run, 4 h of cool white fluorescent light at 50 to 300 lux may be provided. This recipe uses 12 h as a labeled default photoperiod, not a page-cited Stamets value, and maps 20% white to 92 lux, inside that 50 to 300 lux band.
  • Stamets. Paul Stamets, Growing Gourmet and Medicinal Mushrooms, Ten Speed Press. Used here as a general cultivation reference. This recipe does not take a photoperiod or lux number from a cited page in that book.
  • Sakamoto 2018. Yuichi Sakamoto, Influences of environmental factors on fruiting body induction, development and maturation in mushroom-forming fungi, Fungal Biology Reviews 32 (2018). Light is a fruiting cue for many basidiomycetes. Blue light is the usual photomorphogenesis signal. Agaricus bisporus is called out as not needing light for a cap. Flammulina velutipes can fruit in the dark as a long stem with a tiny cap.
