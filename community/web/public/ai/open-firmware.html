Open firmware for Shrooly and Lykyn: AI on top, local control underneath

By Mike Moore · Updated 2026-11-21

Both supported chambers run open source firmware: OpenShrooly on the Shrooly and Shroom Labs firmware on the Lykyn. The firmware holds humidity, fresh air and light on its own, on your Wi-Fi, with no account. Grow Coach and Autopilot only send new targets through an optional, outbound cloud link, so the chamber keeps growing if that link or our site goes down.

Why does open firmware matter for an AI grow chamber?

Because you can read exactly what runs your chamber, and the part that keeps your mushrooms alive never depends on a model or a server.

The firmware runs the control loop: it reads the sensor, switches the mister, cycles the fans and runs the light schedule. Grow Coach sits on top and can only change targets. Chambers whose company app stopped working are the reason this project exists, so local control comes first.

What runs on a Shrooly?

OpenShrooly, free open source firmware started by Graham Stewart, which Shroom Labs builds on, signs, and installs from the browser in about 5 minutes.

You need Chrome or Edge on a computer and a USB-C data cable. The browser checks the release signature before anything is sent to the chamber, and later updates install over Wi-Fi and are checked the same way. Upstream code: grahamsz/openshrooly. Start at the install page.

What runs on a Lykyn?

Native Shroom Labs firmware on the Lykyn's ESP32-C3, validated on a real unit, with a local dashboard, an optional cloud link, grow profiles and Grow Coach.

The Lykyn has an SHT3x temperature and humidity sensor, two PWM fans, an ultrasonic mister and a 16 pixel grow ring, and no heater or cooler, so temperature targets are tracked rather than forced. Back up the stock image before you flash and do not erase the flash. Details on Lykyn for mushrooms and in the firmware repo.

What does the cloud link send and receive?

The chamber sends its readings and a list of what it can do; it receives targets, profiles and schedules, and answers each command with applied or refused.

The link is opt-in and outbound: the chamber calls out to the Shroom Labs API, nothing calls in. A linked chamber proves who it is with a secret it creates on its own chip, and that secret never leaves the chip, so a copied log line cannot impersonate your chamber. You can turn the link off in the chamber's settings. Design notes: Device identity and opt-in cloud control.

What happens when the internet or Shroom Labs goes down?

The chamber keeps running its current targets locally, and Autopilot pauses until fresh readings arrive.

Pending Autopilot commands expire after 35 minutes, so a backlog cannot pile up and land all at once when the link returns. You can still use the chamber's buttons and its local web page on your network.

Can I use Home Assistant instead of the cloud?

Yes. A chamber running OpenShrooly stays fully usable on your network, including from Home Assistant, with no account and no cloud link.

You would then run the grow profile numbers yourself or with your own automations. Grow Coach needs the cloud link because it reads the chamber's history.
