Question

Can AI identify mushrooms safely?

By Mike Moore · Updated 2026-11-21

No, not well enough to decide whether a wild mushroom is safe to eat. In a 2023 Clinical Toxicology study the best of three apps identified 49% of specimens correctly, and a 2026 test of 12 tools found even the best one wrong in nearly 15% of cases. Use an app as a first clue only, confirm with an expert, and in the US call Poison Help at 1-800-222-1222 if someone ate a mushroom you are unsure of.

What did the studies find?

Every published test so far found identification apps too unreliable to rule out a poisonous mushroom on their own.

  • Clinical Toxicology, 2023: three apps were tested on photos of 78 specimens sent to an Australian poisons centre and botanic garden. The most accurate identified 49% correctly, and 44% of the poisonous ones. The authors concluded the apps are not reliable enough to exclude exposure to poisonous mushrooms when used alone.
  • npj Science of Food, 2026: 12 tools were tested on more than 100 real world photos of nearly 60 species. The best performer still failed in nearly 15% of cases, and none consistently gave a single correct answer.
  • Public Citizen, 2024: a consumer group report warned that AI identification tools are being marketed to beginner foragers and that some misidentifications appear to come from regional bias.

Why do photo apps get mushrooms wrong?

Many toxic and edible species look alike in a photo, and young mushrooms can look very different from mature ones.

The 2026 study found that only 3 of 12 tools named fly agaric first when shown it at the button stage, while 10 of 12 got it right when mature. A photo also cannot show spore print color, smell, or what is under the soil, which experts use.

Does Grow Coach's photo check identify mushrooms?

No. It assumes you already know the species because you are growing it from a culinary profile, and it refuses photos that are not a mushroom grow.

It looks at stage and problems such as drying or possible mold on a grow you set up. See photo check and charts.

What is a safer way to get into mushrooms?

Grow a known culinary species from a kit or a block, where you know exactly what you are eating.

Oyster and lion's mane are good first grows. Start with the easiest mushrooms to grow or how to grow mushrooms at home. If you want to forage, learn in person with a local mycological society before eating anything you picked.

What should I do if someone ate a mushroom I am not sure about?

In the US, call Poison Help at 1-800-222-1222 right away, even if nobody feels sick yet, and keep any leftover mushroom or a photo of it.

Poison control is free, expert and confidential. Call 911 if the person collapses, has a seizure, has trouble breathing, or cannot be woken. Source: National Capital Poison Center: Poison Control and the Poison Help line.
