How much humidity and fresh air do mushrooms need?

Updated 2026-10-26

Most culinary mushrooms need 86 to 95% humidity while pins form and 82 to 93% while they fruit, plus regular fresh air to clear the carbon dioxide they breathe out. Too little humidity gives dry, cracked caps and stalled pins. Too little fresh air gives long stems and small caps. The two pull against each other, so the goal is steady humidity with gentle, regular air changes.

What humidity do mushrooms need at each stage?

Humidity is highest while pins form, then drops a little while mushrooms fruit so the caps do not stay wet. These are the numbers each profile uses.

During colonization the bag or block holds its own moisture, so room humidity matters less. Fresh air also matters less then, because mycelium grows well in the carbon dioxide it makes.

Why do mushrooms need fresh air?

Mushrooms breathe in oxygen and breathe out carbon dioxide, like we do. In a closed box carbon dioxide builds up fast. High carbon dioxide tells the mushroom it is still underground, so it stretches its stem to reach open air and keeps its cap small. Fresh air tells it that it has reached the surface and can open its cap.

Oysters are the most sensitive. Pearl Oyster runs a fan run of 40 s every 10 min while fruiting in its profile, while Lion's Mane runs a fan run of 25 s every 18 min.

How do you know if humidity is too low?

  • Pins form and then turn dry, yellow, or stop growing.
  • Caps crack, curl up at the edges, or look leathery.
  • The cut surface of the block looks pale and dry.
  • Lion's mane turns yellow or its spines stay short.

Read dry or cracked mushroom caps for fixes.

How do you know if humidity is too high?

  • Water pools on the caps or the block surface.
  • Caps get yellow or brown sticky spots, a sign of bacteria.
  • Mushrooms feel slimy instead of firm.

The fix is usually more fresh air, not less humidity. Air moving across the surface lets water evaporate, which is what mushrooms need to pull moisture up through the block.

How do you know if fresh air is too low?

  • Long, thin stems with small caps.
  • Oysters that look like coral or tubes instead of shelves.
  • Fuzzy white growth on the stems or caps.
  • A heavy, stale smell when you open the tent or chamber.

Read long, leggy mushroom stems for fixes.

How do you raise humidity without stale air?

  • Mist the walls, not the mushrooms. Two or three times a day in a tent or tub. Fine mist, no puddles.
  • Use a humidity tent. A clear bag or tub over the block with a few holes holds moisture while air still moves.
  • Add damp perlite to the bottom of a tub. It gives off moisture slowly.
  • Use a humidifier on a timer or a chamber. A Shrooly chamber running OpenShrooly holds the profile's humidity and runs its fan on a schedule for each stage.

How do you check your humidity reading?

Cheap hygrometers often read several percent off, and the error is often worst near the top of the range, where mushrooms live. To check one, seal it in a bag or jar with a small cap of table salt mixed with a few drops of water to a damp paste. After 8 to 12 hours at steady room temperature it should read 75%. Note how far off it is and correct your readings by that much.

FAQ

What humidity do oyster mushrooms need?

Pearl Oyster pins at 93% and fruits at 88% humidity in its profile. Oysters also need more fresh air than most species.

Can mushrooms get too much humidity?

Yes. Standing water on caps can cause bacterial spots and soft, slimy mushrooms. More fresh air usually fixes it faster than lowering humidity.

How often should you fan mushrooms?

In a tent or tub, fan two to four times a day, more often for oysters. A chamber runs its fan on a schedule. The oyster profiles here run the fan as often as every 10 minutes while fruiting. Slower species need less.

Do mushrooms need fresh air during colonization?

Very little. Mycelium grows well in the carbon dioxide it makes. Fresh air becomes important when you want pins to form.
