Grow problems

Why do my mushrooms have long stems and small caps?

Updated 2026-10-26

Long, thin stems with small caps almost always mean too much carbon dioxide. The mushrooms are stretching to reach fresh air. Add more air changes, open more holes, or fan more often. If air is good, check light next: mushrooms in a dark corner also stretch toward the nearest light.

What causes leggy mushrooms?

  • Too little fresh air. The most common cause by far. Carbon dioxide builds up around the block and the mushrooms grow stems instead of caps.
  • Too little light. Mushrooms grow toward light. In a dark spot they stretch and the caps stay small and pale.
  • Light from one side. Stems bend and stretch toward a window.
  • Too warm. Some species, especially cool-weather oysters, grow thin and fast in a warm room.

How do you fix long stems?

  1. Add fresh air. Fan a tent four or more times a day, add holes, or prop the lid open a little. In a chamber, check that the fan runs and the profile matches the species.
  2. Give 12 hours of indirect light a day, from above or in front of the opening.
  3. Move the block somewhere a bit cooler if your species likes it cool. Blue Oyster fruits at 14 °C (57 °F) in its profile.
  4. Keep humidity up while you add air. Mist the walls more often if the extra air dries things out.

The current flush may stay leggy, but it is still safe to eat. The next flush should look better.

Which mushrooms show it most?

Oysters show it fastest. Pearl Oyster, Blue Oyster, and Golden Oyster all run their fans hardest in fruiting for this reason. In its profile Pearl Oyster runs a fan run of 40 s every 10 min.

Some species are grown with long stems on purpose. Enoki is grown in the dark with high carbon dioxide to get its thin white stems, and king oyster growers sometimes keep air lower to grow a thick stem.

How do you set up the next flush so it does not stretch?

  • Open the right spot. Mushrooms grow out of the opening. Face it toward the light and toward open air, not into a corner.
  • Do not crowd blocks. Several blocks in one small tent share the same air. Leave space, or add air changes for each block you add.
  • Start fresh air before pins form. Leggy stems start early. Raise air changes when you start fruiting conditions, not when you first notice long stems.
  • Match the profile to the species. In a chamber, an oyster on a lion's mane profile gets less air than it wants. Pearl Oyster pins with a fan run of 35 s every 15 min in its profile.

Is it fresh air or light?

  • Stems long and caps small but normal color: fresh air.
  • Caps pale and stems bending toward one side: light.
  • Fuzzy white growth on the stems: fresh air. This fuzz is mycelium reaching for air, not mold.

FAQ

Are leggy mushrooms safe to eat?

Yes, if they grew from a clean block and look and smell normal. Long stems change texture, not safety. Stems can be tougher, so slice them thin or use them for stock.

Why are my oyster mushrooms shaped like coral?

Coral-like or tube-shaped oysters are a classic sign of too much carbon dioxide. Add fresh air and the next flush should form normal shelves.

Do leggy mushrooms mean the block is bad?

No. It means the conditions around the block need changing. The same block can give a normal flush next time with more air and light.

Can too much fresh air hurt mushrooms?

Strong air blowing straight on the mushrooms dries them out and cracks caps. Aim for regular gentle air changes, not a constant breeze.
