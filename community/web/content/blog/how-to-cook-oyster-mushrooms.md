## How do you cook oyster mushrooms?

Hot pan, one layer, no oil at first, and leave them alone. That is the whole trick. Oyster mushrooms are thin and full of water. USDA FoodData Central lists raw oyster mushrooms at 89.2 g of water per 100 g ([USDA](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/168580/nutrients)). Until most of that water is gone, the pan cannot brown them. If the pan is cool or crowded, the water pools and the mushrooms boil in it. That is where the grey, slippery oysters people complain about come from.

So cook in two phases. First drive the water out with high heat in a dry pan. Then add fat and flavor once they start to brown. Utah State Extension gives the same rule for sautéed mushrooms: cook until the liquid is gone or until golden brown ([Utah State](https://extension.usu.edu/nutrition/research/mushrooms)).

High heat is also a good choice for nutrition. A study that cooked oyster mushrooms (_Pleurotus ostreatus_) and king oysters four ways found grilling and microwaving kept the nutritional profile best, while boiling and deep frying lost more protein and antioxidant activity. The authors point to browning products from the Maillard reaction as part of why grilled mushrooms scored higher ([Roncero-Ramos et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1080/09637486.2016.1244662)). A hot dry sear is close to grilling.

## How to dry sear oyster mushrooms

1. Trim off any substrate at the base, then tear the cluster into bite size pieces along the gills. Do not soak them. Brush off debris, or rinse briefly and pat dry, right before cooking.
2. Heat a heavy skillet, cast iron if you have it, over high heat until it is hot. No oil yet.
3. Add the mushrooms in a single layer with room between pieces. Cook in batches if they do not fit.
4. Press them flat with a spatula or a second pan and leave them. They will squeak, give off water, then go quiet as it boils away.
5. When the edges brown, flip them and brown the other side.
6. Now add a spoon of oil or butter, a pinch of salt and some garlic or thyme. Toss for about a minute until fragrant.
7. Make sure they are hot all the way through, then serve right away. Seared oysters go soft as they sit.

## Can you eat oyster mushrooms raw?

No. Cook them. University of Illinois Extension does not recommend eating specialty or gourmet mushrooms raw and says all of them should be thoroughly cooked. It also says that because mushrooms are not a high risk food like meat or eggs, cooking them to an internal temperature of 145 °F (63 °C) should be enough ([Illinois Extension](https://extension.illinois.edu/mushrooms/preparation)). Ohio State's home grower guide ends the same way: fully cook any fresh mushrooms ([Ohio State](https://mycology.osu.edu/node/11)).

Thin torn pieces heat through fast. Thick blue caps and king oyster stems take a little longer, so check those.

## Should you wash oyster mushrooms?

Only right before you cook them, and only briefly. Illinois Extension notes that USDA does not recommend washing produce, mushrooms included, until just before use, because produce left wet in storage can let bacteria grow ([Illinois Extension](https://extension.illinois.edu/mushrooms/preparation)). Missouri Extension says to clean mushrooms just before using them with a soft brush or a damp paper towel, or a very quick rinse under cold water and a pat dry ([Missouri Extension](https://extension.missouri.edu/sites/default/files/2024-12/n01418.pdf)).

Homegrown oysters from a clean block usually need nothing more than trimming the base. Wet mushrooms also fight you in the pan, so dry them well.

## How do pink, blue and pearl oysters cook differently?

They all take the same dry sear. What changes is texture and how fast you need to use them.

- **Pink oyster** ([profile](/profiles/pink-oyster)) is the meatiest and the chewiest. Ohio State describes it as meaty and seafood like, and says that fried it can taste like bacon or ham ([Ohio State](https://mycology.osu.edu/node/12)). Cook it hard until crisp at the edges. It is the one to cook the day you pick it.
- **Blue oyster** ([profile](/profiles/blue-oyster)) gives the biggest caps when grown cool. A USDA SARE funded grower booklet says one blue strain grown at 50 to 65 °F (10 to 18 °C) makes large, thick caps ([Crosby 2016](https://projects.sare.org/media/pdf/F/u/n/FungiAlly_Oyster_booklet_Final-1.pdf)). Thick caps hold up to a press in the pan, so they sear like a steak.
- **Pearl oyster** ([profile](/profiles/pearl-oyster)) is the classic grocery oyster, _Pleurotus ostreatus_, and the most all purpose. Its clusters tear into even pieces that brown fast.

Golden oyster and king oyster cook the same way. Golden is the most delicate and Ohio State says sautéed in oil it tastes like roasted cashews. King oyster has the thick stem, so slice it into rounds before searing.

Pick at the right stage and every one of them cooks better. Our pearl oyster profile says to harvest when caps flatten and edges just begin to turn up, and Ohio State says picking before the caps curl reduces toughness. [How to fruit an oyster mushroom kit](/blog/how-to-fruit-an-oyster-mushroom-kit) covers harvest timing in detail.

## How long do oyster mushrooms last in the fridge?

It depends on the species, and on the container. Here are the numbers by species.

| Oyster | Fridge, paper bag | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pearl oyster (_P. ostreatus_) | 5 to 7 days | University of Kentucky; 5 to 7 days at 4 °C in a storage review |
| Blue oyster (_P. ostreatus_ var. _columbinus_) | 5 to 7 days, same species as pearl | University of Kentucky; storage review |
| Pink oyster (_P. djamor_) | Under 3 days | Ohio State |
| Golden oyster (_P. citrinopileatus_) | Shorter than pearl or blue, delicate | Ohio State |
| King oyster (_P. eryngii_) | Up to a week | Ohio State |
| Any oyster at room temperature | 1 to 3 days at 20 to 25 °C | Storage review |
| Any oyster in a sealed container | Rots in 1 to 2 days | SARE booklet |

The University of Kentucky puts oyster mushrooms at five to seven days under refrigeration, against 12 to 14 for shiitake, and says to refrigerate them right away in paper bags or vented boxes ([University of Kentucky](https://ccd.uky.edu/sites/default/files/2024-11/ccd-cp-082_shiitake-and-oyster-mushrooms.pdf)). A review of fresh mushroom storage gives _P. ostreatus_ 5 to 7 days at 4 °C and 1 to 3 days at 20 to 25 °C ([Castellanos-Reyes et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/foods10092126)). Ohio State gives pink oyster a shelf life of under 3 days, calls golden exceptionally delicate with a shorter shelf life, and says king oyster can stay fresh for up to a week refrigerated ([Ohio State](https://mycology.osu.edu/node/12)).

Those are outer limits. For best texture, Ohio State says to keep oysters in a paper bag in the fridge, not plastic, and use them within three days, because they wilt, dry out and get tough ([Ohio State](https://mycology.osu.edu/node/11)). Our pink oyster profile says the same thing in fewer words: harvest early, shelf life is short.

## What is the best way to store oyster mushrooms?

A paper bag, or a container with holes, in the coldest part of the fridge. Never sealed plastic. The SARE booklet stores oysters at 36 to 38 °F (2 to 3 °C) with a cover to slow drying and some holes for air, says they can keep about 10 days that way, and says that in a sealed container the water cannot escape and they rot within 1 to 2 days ([Crosby 2016](https://projects.sare.org/media/pdf/F/u/n/FungiAlly_Oyster_booklet_Final-1.pdf)).

Sealed containers are a safety issue too, not just a quality one. Health Canada says storing fresh mushrooms unrefrigerated in an airtight container may promote the growth of bacteria that cause botulism, and says to refrigerate them in a paper bag ([Health Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-safety-fruits-vegetables/mushrooms.html)).

Other rules worth keeping:

- Do not store them at room temperature ([Missouri Extension](https://extension.missouri.edu/sites/default/files/2024-12/n01418.pdf)).
- Do not wash before storing. Wash right before cooking.
- Do not pile them deep. The SARE booklet stacks harvested oysters no more than two high because they are fragile.
- Throw out any that are bruised or spoiled, as Health Canada advises.

## Can you freeze oyster mushrooms?

Yes, but cook them first. Health Canada says mushrooms can be frozen only if they are steamed or sautéed first ([Health Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-safety-fruits-vegetables/mushrooms.html)), and Utah State says fresh mushrooms should never be frozen. Sear them, cool them, and freeze them flat in a bag. Missouri Extension says cooked mushrooms are best used within 1 month for quality ([Missouri Extension](https://extension.missouri.edu/sites/default/files/2024-12/n01418.pdf)).

Drying is the long keeper. The University of Kentucky says drying, then storing in sealed containers, adds at least six months of shelf life.

## What are three simple oyster mushroom dishes for Thanksgiving?

All three start with the dry sear above. Do the searing close to serving time, since seared oysters lose their crisp edges as they sit.

### 1. Seared oysters with butter, garlic and thyme

Best with pearl or blue oyster. Dry sear the mushrooms in batches until browned. Put them all back in the pan, add butter, a chopped garlic clove and a few thyme leaves, and toss until the butter foams. Finish with salt, pepper and a squeeze of lemon. Serve as a side, or spoon over mashed potatoes or sliced turkey. Utah State notes a little lemon juice also helps mushrooms keep their color.

### 2. Crispy pink oyster bits for green beans or salad

Best with pink oyster, the one Ohio State says can taste like bacon or ham when fried. Tear it small, dry sear until the water is gone, then add a little more oil than usual and keep cooking, pressing the pieces flat, until they are crisp and dark at the edges. Salt them, drain on a paper towel, and scatter over green beans, a salad or a squash soup right before serving. If you grow pink for color, our Chef's line [bright pink oyster](/profiles/pink-oyster-bright) profile is built for that; see the [Chef's line](/chefs-line) for the rest.

### 3. Oyster mushroom gravy

Any oyster works. Sear a pan of torn oysters until well browned, then add butter and an equal amount of flour and stir for a minute. Whisk in stock or turkey drippings a little at a time until it is as thick as you like, and simmer a few minutes. Searing first matters here. Utah State says to sauté mushrooms before adding them to a stew or braise, because they shrink as they cook. The browning is where the gravy gets its flavor.

For a crowd, roasting is easier than batches in a skillet. Utah State roasts mushrooms with salt, pepper and garlic at 400 °F (204 °C) until golden brown. Spread oysters in one layer on a sheet pan with space between pieces, the same as in the skillet, so they roast instead of steam.

## How long do cooked oyster mushrooms keep?

Treat them like any other leftover. USDA says to refrigerate cooked food within 2 hours, keep leftovers 3 to 4 days in the fridge, and reheat them to 165 °F (74 °C). Bring gravy to a rolling boil when you reheat it ([USDA FSIS](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)). On a long Thanksgiving table, that 2 hour limit is the one people miss.

## FAQ

### Why are my oyster mushrooms rubbery?

They steamed instead of seared. The pan was too crowded or not hot enough, so the water they gave off had nowhere to go. Cook in a hot dry pan in a single layer, in batches, and wait until the water is gone before you add fat.

### Do you eat the stems of oyster mushrooms?

Yes, on pearl, blue and pink, though the base where the cluster joins can be tough. Trim off any substrate and the hardest part of the base. King oyster is grown for its thick stem.

### How do you cook pink oyster mushrooms?

Hot and fast, the day you harvest them. Tear them small, dry sear until the edges are crisp, then add oil and salt. Pink has the chewiest texture of the three, and Ohio State says fried it can taste like bacon or ham. Eat it within 3 days at most.

### Do pink oyster mushrooms stay pink when cooked?

Do not count on it. Our pink oyster profile notes the color already fades as the mushrooms mature, so harvest early while it is vivid. Grow pink for the flavor and the texture first.

### Can you eat oyster mushrooms raw?

No. Illinois Extension does not recommend eating specialty mushrooms raw. Cook them through, to at least 145 °F (63 °C) inside.

### Should you store oyster mushrooms in plastic?

No. Use a paper bag or a vented container in the fridge. In a sealed container they hold their own water and can rot within 1 to 2 days.

### Which oyster mushroom should I grow for cooking?

Grow the one that fits your room. Blue for a cool room, pink for a warm one, pearl for anything in between.
