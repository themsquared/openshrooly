## Which oyster mushroom should you grow: blue, pink or golden?

The one that likes the temperature your room already has. All three are oyster mushrooms in the genus _Pleurotus_, all three fruit from the same kind of hardwood or straw block, and all three want damp air, plenty of fresh air and some light. The big difference is heat. Blue oyster wants it cool, pink oyster wants it warm, and golden oyster sits between them.

That matters more than any other choice you make. A small chamber or a tote on a counter mostly follows the room. Our own chamber profiles say it plainly: there is no heater or cooler, so a temperature target only holds if the room sits near it. Fighting your room for three weeks is how a first grow goes wrong.

- **Cool room**, about 10 to 18 °C (50 to 64 °F): blue oyster.
- **Normal to warm room**, about 18 to 24 °C (64 to 75 °F): golden oyster, kept indoors.
- **Warm room or summer with no air conditioning**, 24 °C (75 °F) and up: pink oyster.
- **First grow ever**: blue oyster, unless your room is warm. Then pink.

All three profiles use the same light: dark while colonizing, then 12 hours a day at about 92 lux with a blue heavy mix. The fan schedule is the same too, about 40 seconds every 10 minutes while fruiting. So the only setting you really choose by species is temperature. [Do mushrooms need light?](/blog/do-mushrooms-need-light) explains the light numbers.

Note the published ranges run warmer than our profiles for pink and golden. Ohio State lists both at 24 to 32 °C (75 to 90 °F) for fruiting. Our profiles sit at the cool end of each range on purpose, where the color holds and the stems stay short, and the warning bands show how far the room can drift before it matters.

## Which oyster grows best in a cold room?

Blue oyster. Its profile pins at 15 °C (59 °F) and fruits at 14 °C (57 °F), and the profile only warns once the room drops below 10 °C (50 °F) or climbs above 20 °C (68 °F). A winter basement, a cool garage, or a spare room with the heat off are all fine.

Cool air is also what keeps it blue. The profile note says the deep color holds better below 18 °C (64 °F), and in a warm chamber blues fade toward grey and the stems stretch. A USDA SARE funded grower booklet says the same about one blue strain: best grown at 50 to 65 °F (10 to 18 °C), with large caps that come in thick and a frosty color when grown cool ([Crosby 2016](https://projects.sare.org/media/pdf/F/u/n/FungiAlly_Oyster_booklet_Final-1.pdf)).

Blue oyster is not one fixed thing. Ohio State separates cold blue strains, which fruit at 35 to 50 °F (2 to 10 °C) and need a cold shock, from warm blue strains that fruit at 60 to 85 °F (16 to 29 °C) and fade from blue to steel grey as they mature ([Ohio State](https://mycology.osu.edu/node/12)). Our profile is set for the common middle: a 14 °C (57 °F) fruiting room.

Do not try pink in a cold room. Its profile summary says it stalls and fades in the cold.

## Which oyster grows best in a warm room?

Pink oyster. It is a tropical species. Its profile colonizes at 27 °C (81 °F), the warmest of the three, and fruits at 24 °C (75 °F), with no warning until the room passes 32 °C (90 °F). A 2024 study of pink oyster found the mycelium grew best at 24 to 28 °C and the species grew well up to 30 °C ([Chowdhury et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.15376/biores.19.1.1696-1706)). A summer apartment with no air conditioning suits it.

Golden oyster is the other warm room option and the better fit if the room sits right around 21 °C (70 °F), which is where its profile fruits. Its warning band runs 16 to 26 °C (61 to 79 °F), so it handles a normal heated home.

There is a limit. The same SARE booklet ran a summer grow in a barn where the room swung between 75 and 95 °F (24 and 35 °C) and called that extremely hot for most mushrooms, likely part of why its yellow oyster yields were poor ([Crosby 2016](https://projects.sare.org/media/pdf/F/u/n/FungiAlly_Oyster_booklet_Final-1.pdf)). Past about 30 °C (86 °F), even pink is near its edge.

## Which oyster is best for a beginner?

Blue oyster, if your room is cool to normal. Ohio State names pearl or blue oyster as the easy starting point, with a wide range of growing conditions from 50 to 80 °F (10 to 27 °C) ([Ohio State](https://mycology.osu.edu/node/11)). Blue keeps longest after harvest, so a picking day that slips by one day is not a loss.

Pink oyster is the beginner pick for a warm room. It is the fastest profile in our library at 20 days, and Ohio State notes it grows on cardboard, paper, coffee grounds and pasteurized straw. [Grow oyster mushrooms on coffee grounds](/blog/grow-oyster-mushrooms-on-coffee-grounds) covers that route. The catch is timing. Pink has to be picked young and eaten fast.

Golden oyster is the one we do not tag as beginner. It is not hard to fruit, but it is fragile, it needs plenty of fresh air to keep stems short, and it carries the invasive species problem below. If your stems run long on any of these three, [leggy mushroom stems](/guides/leggy-mushroom-stems) walks through the fixes.

## How long does each one take to grow?

Pink is fastest, golden and blue are close behind. From the start of colonization to the end of the first harvest, the profiles run 20 days for pink, 24 for golden and 25 for blue. From a kit that arrives fully colonized, cut that to 10, 12 and 13 days. Grower guides land in the same place. The SARE booklet puts the harvest about 5 to 10 days after a block goes into fruiting conditions, and Ohio State says pink fruits in 7 to 10 days.

## How long do blue, pink and golden oysters keep after harvest?

Blue keeps longest, pink keeps shortest, and golden is close to pink.

- **Blue oyster.** Oyster mushrooms keep 5 to 7 days in the fridge, per the University of Kentucky. A review of fresh mushroom storage gives _P. ostreatus_, the species blue oyster belongs to, 5 to 7 days at 4 °C and 1 to 3 days at 20 to 25 °C ([Castellanos-Reyes et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/foods10092126)). The SARE booklet says oysters stored at 36 to 38 °F (2 to 3 °C) in a container that breathes can keep about 10 days.
- **Pink oyster.** Ohio State gives it a shelf life of under 3 days, and the SARE booklet calls its storage and shelf life limited, good for a farmers market but not for wholesale. Our profile says to harvest early and while the color is still vivid, because the pink fades as the fruits mature.
- **Golden oyster.** Ohio State calls it exceptionally delicate with a shorter shelf life. Plan to cook it the day you pick it.

For all three, Ohio State's advice is a paper bag in the fridge, not plastic, and use within three days for the best texture. Harvest before the cap edges flatten or flip up. The SARE booklet says a slight roll on the edge keeps the best texture and storability, and limits spore release.

## What do blue, pink and golden oysters taste like?

Each one cooks differently, which is a good reason to grow more than one.

- **Pink** is the meatiest. Ohio State describes a meaty, seafood like, umami taste and a chewy texture that can taste like bacon or ham when fried.
- **Golden** is the most fragrant. Ohio State ranks it among the tastier oysters and says sautéed in oil it tastes like roasted cashews. Michigan's invasive species program calls the flavor nutty.
- **Blue** is the classic oyster, and the one that holds up as a big cap. Grown cool, its caps come in large and thick.

Light changes golden oyster too. A 2025 study grew golden oyster under 12 hours of pure blue light and got stems 12.5% shorter than under white light, fuller color, and more of the umami and sweet amino acids. Red and far red light gave pale, soft mushrooms ([Chen et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnut.2025.1554575)). Our [golden oyster deep yellow](/profiles/golden-oyster-deep-yellow) profile is built on that result.

## Is golden oyster invasive?

Yes, in North America. Golden oyster is native to eastern Asia. A 2025 study by the University of Wisconsin and the USDA Forest Service Center for Forest Mycology Research found it escaped into North American forests around 2010 and has since been reported in 25 US states and one Canadian province. In dead elm trees where it grew, the mix of fungi changed and the number of fungal species dropped significantly ([Veerabahu et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2025.06.049)). Michigan's invasive species program summarizes it as about half the expected diversity of native fungi, and reports golden oyster growing wild in Michigan's southern Lower Peninsula ([Michigan Invasive Species Program](https://www.michigan.gov/invasives/news/2025/08/05/popular-golden-oyster-mushrooms-now-invading-michigan-forests)).

Ohio State does not recommend it for outdoor cultivation in North America. The study's lead author asks sellers to note that it should be grown indoors and not composted. If you grow it:

- Grow it indoors only. No logs, no garden beds, no outdoor fruiting.
- Do not compost the spent block or dump it outside. Seal it in a bag and put it in the trash.
- Harvest before the caps flatten. The SARE booklet notes that picking at that stage also limits spore release.

If you would rather skip that, blue and pink oysters give you the cool room and warm room options without it.

## FAQ

### Which oyster mushroom is easiest to grow?

Blue oyster for a cool or normal room, pink oyster for a warm one. Ohio State names pearl and blue as the easy starting point, and both blue and pink are tagged beginner in our profile library.

### Is pink oyster faster than blue oyster?

Yes. Pink runs 20 days from spawn to the end of the first harvest in its profile, against 25 for blue. From a colonized kit, it is 10 days for pink and 13 for blue.

### Can you put a pink oyster block in the fridge?

No. Pink oyster is tropical, its profile says never to cold shock it, and the SARE grower booklet says the pink strain it grew cannot survive temperatures below 40 °F (4 °C). Keep the block warm and eat the harvest within a couple of days.

### Why are my blue oysters turning grey?

The room is too warm. Blue oyster holds its color best below about 18 °C (64 °F), and its profile fruits at 14 °C (57 °F). Some warm blue strains also turn steel grey as they mature, so pick them young.

### Can you grow blue, pink and golden oyster at the same time?

Not well in one space. Their fruiting temperatures are 10 °C (18 °F) apart, 14 °C (57 °F) for blue and 24 °C (75 °F) for pink. Grow them one after another as the seasons change, blue in winter and pink in summer.

### Is golden oyster safe to grow at home?

It is a common edible, but it is invasive in North American forests. Grow it indoors only and put the spent block in the trash, not the compost.
