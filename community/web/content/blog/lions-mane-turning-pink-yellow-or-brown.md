## What each lion's mane color means

Lion's mane should be white. Every other color is the mushroom telling you something about light, age, air or handling. The trick is that the same color can show up for two different reasons, so look at where it is and when it showed up.

| Color | Where and when | Most likely cause | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pink or peach blush | Young head, fades a little as it matures | Light stress, strain, cool room | Cut light to about 200 lux, 12 h a day |
| Pale yellow, spine tips | Mature head, long spines | Age | Harvest now |
| Yellow on the side facing a fan or vent | Any age, one side | Dry or drafty air | Less fresh air, more humidity |
| Yellow, wet or slimy patches | Any age, with drips on the bag | Too wet, too little airflow | Mist less, move more air |
| Brown spots or streaks | Where it was touched or pressed | Bruising | Trim and cook soon |
| Brown all over, after picking | In the fridge or on the counter | Storage browning | Store colder and use sooner |
| Brown, dry, crusty tips | On the block | Very dry air | Raise humidity |

The rest of this post walks through each one with the numbers behind it.

## Why is my lion's mane turning pink?

Pink on lion's mane is mostly a light and strain reaction, not rot. The best data on it comes from a 2025 study at the Czech University of Life Sciences that grew four lion's mane strains ([Jozifek et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/agriculture15050460)):

- One of the four strains stayed pure white in every trial. The other three showed pink while the heads were young.
- The pink got slightly weaker as the heads matured.
- Their grow room ran 17 to 18 °C with 500 lux of light 24 hours a day. Even the control bags, with nothing added to the substrate, came out slightly pink.
- When they dropped the light to 200 lux or less on a 12 hour cycle, the heads came out white. A run at only 35 lux, even with light on all 24 hours, was also completely white.
- The authors name the cool temperature, high light and nonstop light as possible causes and call the pink a possible stress reaction that depends on the strain. They note other researchers grew lion's mane under 1,000 lux without any pink, which points back at strain.

The pink in that study got stronger when they added selenium to the substrate, and an older study also saw lion's mane go white pink when selenium salts were added ([Niedzielski et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1080/03601234.2014.951576)). A store kit or a normal hardwood block has no added selenium, so at home the lever you control is light.

Our [dense white heads profile](/profiles/lions-mane-dense-head) goes further, at about 46 lux, for growers who want shade and tight heads. Its notes say a cooler room can still give a faint pink blush.

### Is pink lion's mane safe to eat?

The study treats pink as a color response to growing conditions, not as spoilage. If the head is firm, dry to the touch and smells mild and fresh, the pink is a looks issue. Judge it the way you would any fresh mushroom: by firmness, smell and slime, covered in the section on when to throw it out below. Pink is not the same as the slow browning and softening of an old head.

## Why is my lion's mane turning yellow?

Yellow has three different causes, and where it shows up tells you which one.

**Yellow spine tips on a big head means age.** Lion's mane starts white and turns yellowish, then brown, as it ages ([Silva et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/horticulturae11020158)). The Minnesota DNR says lion's mane is best eaten white and that yellowing means aging and can taste bitter ([Minnesota DNR](https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/mcvmagazine/issues/2018/sep-oct/lions-mane.html)). Our profile note is to harvest when the spines lengthen but before they yellow. If yours is yellowing now, cut it today. Next flush, pick a day or two sooner.

**Yellow on one side means dry or moving air.** Our profile's chamber note is direct about this: too much fresh air yellows the spines, and if the spines brown or yellow, cut fresh air and raise humidity a little. The profile runs the fan only 25 seconds every 18 minutes while fruiting, at 90% humidity. A kit right next to a heater vent, a window draft or a desk fan gets far more moving air than that.

**Yellow, wet patches mean too much water.** NCAT's ATTRA guide, a USDA funded grower program, says yellow patches or slimy spots with constant condensation mean humidity is too high or airflow too low, and links them to bacterial blotch ([NCAT ATTRA 2026](https://www.ncat.org/publication/mushroom-cultivation/)). That yellow looks glossy and wet, not dry and papery. Mist the air, not the head, and give it a little more air movement.

## Why is my lion's mane turning brown?

Brown is browning: enzymes in the mushroom react with its own phenolic compounds once tissue is damaged or aging. In lion's mane, a 2025 storage study found the activity of the browning enzymes polyphenol oxidase, tyrosinase and laccase rose sharply during storage ([Postharvest Biology and Technology 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.postharvbio.2025.113942)). Three things set it off.

**Bruising.** Lion's mane spines are very tender. A study on fresh lion's mane browning notes they are easily damaged during harvest and storage, which leads to browning ([IIR 2016](https://doi.org/10.18462/iir.iccc.2016.0049)). Brown spots where you held it, pressed it against the bag or stacked it are bruises. Handle the head by the base, not the spines.

**Warm storage.** This is the big one after picking. A 2025 study stored harvested lion's mane at 5, 13 and 21 °C for up to 14 days ([Silva et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/horticulturae11020158)):

| Stored at | Weight loss | Color change by day 4 | Condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 °C (41 °F) | About 8% by day 7 | 3.0, stayed under 7 all week | Microbe counts acceptable until day 7 |
| 13 °C (55 °F) | Past 10% by day 4 | 6.1 | Middle ground |
| 21 °C (70 °F) | About 28% in 2 days | 13.5 | Visible spoilage by day 7 |

The color change number is a standard lab measure where 3 to 6 is noticeable, 6 to 12 is significant and over 12 is considerable. The authors also found lion's mane browned faster than gray oyster mushrooms even under refrigeration, so it is a mushroom to eat early. NCAT gives the same advice from the farm side: cool mushrooms fast to 34 to 38 °F (1 to 3 °C), store them in a paper bag or vented container, and do not seal them in plastic ([NCAT ATTRA 2026](https://www.ncat.org/publication/mushroom-cultivation/)).

**Dry air on the block.** Brown, crusty tips on a head that is still growing usually mean the air dried them out. That is the same moisture problem as [dry, cracked caps](/guides/dry-cracked-mushroom-caps) on other species, and NCAT says lion's mane is especially sensitive to low humidity ([NCAT ATTRA 2026](https://www.ncat.org/publication/mushroom-cultivation/)).

## When is discolored lion's mane still good to cook?

Color alone does not decide it. Firmness, smell and slime do.

- **Keep and cook:** a firm head with a pink blush, a few yellow tips or a small brown bruise. Trim the bruised or yellow parts. Yellowing heads may taste bitter, so the earlier you cook it, the better.
- **Trim hard:** a head with brown, dried out edges but a firm, white center.
- **Throw it out:** slimy or wet patches, a sour or off smell, fuzzy mold, or a head that has gone soft and brown all through. NCAT links slimy spots to bacterial blotch, and the 2025 storage study saw visible spoilage by day 7 at room temperature ([Silva et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/horticulturae11020158)). If you are not sure, do not eat it.

## How to keep your next lion's mane flush white

1. Set light to about 200 lux for 12 hours a day, or less. Room light or a bright window with no direct sun is enough. Our [lion's mane profile](/profiles/lions-mane) runs 190 lux on a 12 hour cycle.
2. Keep the room in the 16 to 20 °C (60 to 68 °F) range NCAT gives for fruiting. Our profile fruits at 17 °C (63 °F).
3. Keep humidity at about 90% while fruiting and 94% while pinning, the settings in our profile, inside the 85 to 95% NCAT recommends.
4. Give it gentle fresh air, not a draft. Our profile runs 25 seconds of fan every 18 minutes. Move the kit away from vents and fans. The [fresh air calculator](/tools/fresh-air-planner) sizes it for a closed chamber.
5. Harvest when the spines have grown out but before the tips yellow. Lift and cut by the base so you never squeeze the spines.
6. Cool it right away. Store it at 34 to 38 °F (1 to 3 °C) in a paper bag, and cook it within a few days.

If the pink comes back under 200 lux and 12 hours, the strain is probably one that blushes. Our [dense white heads profile](/profiles/lions-mane-dense-head) runs about 46 lux, which is closer to the 35 lux run that came out fully white in the 2025 study.

## Why does light change lion's mane color at all?

Lion's mane uses light as a signal to form and shape the head, not for energy. That is why it needs some light but not much. In the 2025 study, the brighter treatment also pulled more selenium into the outer layer of the head, the side facing the light ([Jozifek et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/agriculture15050460)). The authors suggest light hitting the surface directly may drive that. They call their light results a pilot and say more work on light intensity and color is needed, so treat 200 lux and 12 hours as a tested starting point, not a law. [Do mushrooms need light?](/blog/do-mushrooms-need-light) has the light numbers for 20 species, and [humidity and fresh air](/guides/humidity-and-fresh-air) explains how damp air and fresh air trade off.

## FAQ

### Why is my lion's mane pink?

Usually light stress, and only on some strains. A 2025 study got pink young heads at 500 lux for 24 hours a day and white heads at 200 lux or less on a 12 hour cycle. The pink faded slightly as the heads matured.

### Is pink lion's mane bad?

The study treats it as a stress color, not spoilage. A firm, fresh smelling pink head is a looks issue. Slime, a sour smell or mold are the signs to throw it out, whatever the color.

### Why is my lion's mane turning yellow?

Age if it is the spine tips on a mature head, dry or drafty air if it is one side, and too much water if the yellow patches are wet or slimy. Harvest yellowing heads now, since yellowing lion's mane can taste bitter.

### Why did my lion's mane turn brown in the fridge?

Enzymatic browning from age or bruising. It goes much faster when warm: in a 2025 study, lion's mane at 21 °C changed color past the considerable line by day 4, while at 5 °C it stayed close to white for a week.

### How do I stop lion's mane from bruising?

Handle it by the base, never squeeze the spines, do not stack heads, and cool it quickly in a paper bag rather than sealed plastic.

### How long does fresh lion's mane last?

About a week in the fridge. A 2025 study kept microbe counts acceptable until day 7 at 5 °C, and NCAT says most fresh mushrooms last 5 to 7 days, lion's mane sometimes a little longer.
