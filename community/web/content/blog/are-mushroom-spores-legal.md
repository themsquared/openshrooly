## Short answer

Spores are not a controlled substance under federal law. Psilocybin and psilocin are. The gap between those two facts is where the state laws live.

This post is a research summary, not legal advice. I checked every row below against a statute, a legislature page, an agency page or a dated legal review on 2026-11-09. Shroom Labs does not sell spores, cultures or psilocybin products, and nothing here is a cultivation guide. If you plan to act on any of it, read the statute for your state and talk to a lawyer.

## What does federal law say about spores?

Federal law lists the chemicals, not the fungus. 21 CFR 1308.11(d) puts psilocybin (code 7437) and psilocyn (code 7438) in Schedule I. The text does not name spores, mycelium or mushrooms.

DEA addressed spores directly in a 2014 letter from its Drug and Chemical Evaluation Section. The position, as summarized by the Legislative Analysis and Public Policy Association (LAPPA), is that spores are not Schedule I because they do not contain psilocybin or psilocin. The same summary notes the other half of the position: once spores germinate and grow into mushrooms that contain psilocybin, that material is a controlled substance under the Controlled Substances Act.

So the federal line sits at the mushroom, not at the spore. That is also why intent matters so much in practice. A spore print on a microscope slide and a spore syringe sold for growing are the same biology, but a state legislature can treat them very differently.

## Which states make spores illegal?

Four states address psilocybin spores or mycelium directly. I read the text for each one.

- **Idaho.** Idaho Code 37-2705(d)(34) lists "spores or mycelium capable of producing mushrooms that contain psilocybin or psilocin" in Schedule I, next to psilocybin and psilocyn themselves. The section was last amended in 2026 (chapter 90) and the spore line is still there. This is the strictest spore rule I found, because the spores are the scheduled substance.
- **Georgia.** O.C.G.A. 16-13-71 defines "dangerous drug" to include mushroom spores that contain psilocybin or psilocin when mature. Section 16-13-72 makes it unlawful to sell, give away, barter, exchange, distribute or possess a dangerous drug outside the licensed channels. Spores are not a Schedule I controlled substance in Georgia, but there is no microscopy carve out in the text.
- **California.** Health and Safety Code 11390 makes it a crime to cultivate spores or mycelium with intent to produce psilocybin or psilocin. Section 11391 covers transporting, importing, selling, furnishing or giving them away to facilitate that cultivation. Both carry up to a year in county jail or state prison. The code also allows approved research, instruction or analysis through the state Research Advisory Panel.
- **Florida.** Fla. Stat. 500.75, in effect since July 1, 2025, makes it a first degree misdemeanor to transport, import, sell, furnish or give away spores or mycelium capable of producing mushrooms that will contain psilocybin or psilocyn. Unlike California, the text does not mention intent. Possession is not on the list of prohibited acts.

## Is psilocybin itself legal anywhere in the US?

Not in the sense most people mean. No state has legalized general private use. What exists is a mix of decriminalization, deprioritization and tightly regulated programs, and psilocybin remains Schedule I federally in all of them.

LAPPA's November 2025 summary counts Schedule I status in 47 states, the District of Columbia and the five territories. Alaska puts all psychedelics in Schedule II and Maine uses its own Schedule X. Oregon and New Mexico exempt psilocybin handled under their regulated programs.

## What changed in 2026?

A few things worth knowing, all from the sources in the table.

- **Colorado narrowed its personal use protection.** HB26-1325 says personal use does not cover selling natural medicine for pay, advertising it, or using paid harm reduction services to run sales. Sharing with adults 21 and older in the listed contexts stays protected.
- **Georgia passed two psychedelic bills that do not legalize anything.** One excepts an FDA approved form of psilocybin from Schedule I only if federal approval and rescheduling happen. The other sets clinic rules. Psilocybin stays Schedule I.
- **California still prohibits psilocybin.** Two 2026 bills that name it were held in committee, and city level deprioritization does not change state law.
- **Idaho kept its spore line** through a 2026 amendment of the Schedule I section.

## Why is the spore question confusing?

Most "spores are legal" pages repeat the federal point and stop. Three things get lost.

1. **Federal silence is not state permission.** Four states wrote spore rules and they are not alike. Idaho treats spores as the drug. Florida and California regulate what you do with them.
2. **The offense is usually the mushroom, not the spore.** Where spores are lawful to hold, a grown cubensis or cyanescens flush is still a Schedule I material. The spore is legal only until it is used.
3. **Sellers do not decide your exposure.** A vendor's "for microscopy only" label does not change what your state's statute says, so I would not assume it protects a buyer.

## FAQ

### Are psilocybin mushroom spores legal federally?

Yes in the narrow sense that they are not listed in the federal schedules. 21 CFR 1308.11 names psilocybin and psilocyn, not spores, and a 2014 DEA letter says spores are not Schedule I because they contain neither compound. Growing them into mushrooms that contain psilocybin creates a controlled substance.

### Which states ban psilocybin spores?

Four: California, Florida, Georgia and Idaho. Idaho lists spores and mycelium as Schedule I. Georgia classes them as a dangerous drug. California and Florida criminalize selling, giving away or transporting them, and California also criminalizes cultivating them to produce psilocybin.

### Is it legal to own spores for microscopy?

Federally nothing in the schedules covers spores. In Idaho and Georgia the statutes have no microscopy exception that I could find. In California and Florida the text targets cultivation and sales or transport, not possession alone, and California separately allows approved research. State law and enforcement vary, so check your state's statute.

### Is psilocybin legal in Colorado and Oregon?

Both run regulated programs, but they work differently. Colorado allows adults 21 and older personal use, possession and cultivation, plus licensed healing centers, and its 2026 update bars sales for pay and advertising. Oregon allows psilocybin only through licensed service centers under ORS 475A. Both remain subject to federal Schedule I status.

### Are culinary mushroom spores legal everywhere?

Spores of culinary species such as oyster, lion's mane and shiitake do not contain psilocybin, and none of the spore statutes above reach them. Georgia's rule, for example, applies to spores of mushrooms that contain psilocybin or psilocin when mature.

### Does a city decriminalization resolution protect me?

No. In California, six cities told police to treat psilocybin as a low priority, but the state's own law still applies and a city resolution is not a defense in a state prosecution.
